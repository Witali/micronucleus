## Set the EOP recovery bit directly

Replace the final ORI/OUT pair with SBI PORTD,D- and retain the existing
two-clock alignment jump. Classic-AVR SBI takes two clocks and preserves
SREG; see the [Microchip instruction manual, SBI](https://www.microchip.com/content/dam/mchp/documents/MCU08/ProductDocuments/ReferenceManuals/AVR-InstructionSet-Manual-DS40002198.pdf).
Two alignment clocks, seventeen countdown clocks and the two-clock write
still produce exactly 21 clocks of driven SE0, followed by 11 clocks of
driven J. R16 is dead after EOP; all unrelated PORTD bits are preserved.

other cold branches. This removes 30 bytes without removing the early EOP
guard, changing the dense terminal polls or weakening PID/ownership checks.

## Replace the redundant receive countdown with the buffer pointer

The combined receiver already computes packet length from Y at EOF. Its R23
countdown only selects the eleventh byte's terminal continuation. Before
bit6, ten bytes have been committed exactly when Y equals RX_BUFFER+10.
Replace each of the three countdown decrements with this pointer comparison.
The following branches use Z; the differing carry is overwritten before the
next payload ROR and cannot change decoding or fractional scheduling.

Initialize R21 in the pre-PID NOP slot. The old adjacent R21/R23 initializers
then become one two-clock jump. This saves two bytes while preserving every
input observation, including the independent ACK reader's entry. That reader
still sets its own one-byte count at bit7; EOF computes the normal count.
